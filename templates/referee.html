For anyone who forecasts football and would rather know

Seal your own record

The machinery on this site does not care whose predictions it measures. Seal yours before kickoff and they go into a hash chain under exactly the same rules as ours — sealed, anchored, never rewritten — and the record is published whichever way it goes. That last part is the whole offer. Anyone can show you a screenshot of a winner.

What you get, precisely

The same rules as ours

One chain per person, under guests/<you>/. Each entry carries the SHA-256 of the one before it, gets an OpenTimestamps proof anchored into a Bitcoin block, and is verified by the same standalone verifier anyone can run against your chain without installing anything. First publication wins. Nothing is ever rewritten, including by you.

Measured by closing line value

Your price against the market-average closing price for the same selection. CLV is the accepted evidence of edge and it converges in hundreds of bets where profit is still noise. Flat-stakes profit and loss is shown too, labelled as the noisier number. Where a sealed Asian-handicap line and the closing line differ, the entry is settled and shown but no CLV is claimed — unlike lines are not divided as though they were the same bet.

What you do not get

No editing. No deleting a bad week. No "since I changed my approach" restart. No ROI headline. If you seal a month of losers, this site publishes a month of losers under your name, and the point of coming here is that a reader knows that before they look.

Coverage

38 competitions — everything football-data.co.uk publishes a closing price for, and far past the {{ n_published }} divisions our own model prices. Twenty-two European leagues carry 1X2, over/under 2.5 and the main Asian handicap; sixteen further countries, from Argentina and Brazil to Japan and the USA, carry 1X2. Run python -m proofodds.guest coverage for the exact matrix. A selection with no closing benchmark cannot be given CLV and we will say so rather than invent one.

If you built a model

Most of the people who could use this are not selling anything. They have a model and no way to find out whether it is any good.

A backtest cannot tell you. It is fitted, tuned and evaluated on the same history, and every mistake that produces — a feature that leaked the result, a hyperparameter chosen on the test set, a season kept because it looked better — makes the number go up rather than down. That is the failure mode: the backtest is wrong in the flattering direction, and it is wrong quietly.

The only honest answer is out-of-sample, forward, against a benchmark you cannot argue with. That is exactly what this machinery does, and it is what we point at our own model — which is how we can tell you it loses, and by how much, with an interval around it. Seal a season of forecasts and you get the same treatment: graded against the market-average close, reported with the sample size and the confidence interval, and no headline claimed from a number too small to carry one.

A record opened in 2026 is free and stays free. A paid plan for people who sell picks is planned and does not exist yet: nothing is charged today, and nobody has been asked to pay.

How it works

You ask for a record, we send you a private token, and from then on you seal your own picks through a form that takes about twenty seconds. Nobody at ProofOdds touches an entry on its way in. There is no open sign-up: tokens are issued by hand, because a record under a name should belong to the person who uses that name.

competition   E0
match         Arsenal v Chelsea, 2026-10-17 14:00 UTC
market        1X2
selection     H
odds taken    2.10

That is the whole entry. Pick and price, sealed before the whistle, nothing else — no reasoning to be judged on, no stake to be argued about. You get a receipt with the entry's hash the moment it is sealed.

If you sell your picks

A pick your subscribers paid for cannot be on a public page before the match. Choose reveal at kickoff and the entry is sealed, chained and timestamped now, and published when the match starts. Until then only its hash and its Bitcoin timestamp proof are public, which is enough to prove later that it existed when it did. Two limits, both deliberate: the match must be within seven days, and entries become public in the order they were sealed, so one held entry holds back those sealed after it.

Sealed before kickoff, checked against the results feed

The kickoff you type is not trusted. When the match is graded, the time you sealed is compared with the kickoff time in the results feed. An entry sealed at or after it is shown on your record as late and is never scored. Where the feed gives no kickoff time, an entry sealed on the match day is shown as time unverified and is not scored either.

Why you might not want this

Worth saying plainly, because a page that only lists reasons to say yes is the sort of page this site exists to be the opposite of.

It can only hurt a bad record

If you do not have an edge, this will show that, in public, permanently, and there is no mechanism for taking it down. That is not a side effect we are apologising for; it is the product.

It is slow

CLV converges faster than profit and it is still not fast. A few hundred entries before the number means much, which is months of ordinary volume. Nothing here will settle an argument this week.
