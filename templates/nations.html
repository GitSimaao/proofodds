The model, pointed at national teams.

Every other page on this site publishes a number that was committed before kickoff and measured afterwards. This one does not. It is the same fitted model applied to international football, shown in full because it is more interesting shown than hidden — and kept out of the record, because it has not done the two things the record is made of.

Not sealed, and not scored. These probabilities are not written to the prediction ledger, carry no hash, and have no external timestamp. Nothing here is graded against a closing line, and not one figure on this page enters the scorecard or any pooled log loss anywhere on this site. A page rebuild recomputes them from the current fit, so the number you are reading is today's, and there is no record of what it said last week. Treat them as the model thinking out loud. The sealed record is the divisions, and it is a separate thing.

How it was fitted

Three decisions that are not the ones the club divisions use. Each is a place the model could be wrong, so each is stated rather than buried.

The pool is wider than the competition

Fitting on the Nations League alone would look like a simplification and be a mistake. Its groups are stratified by ability into Leagues A to D, so a team meets only opponents of its own level and a weak side that beats weak peers reads as strong. Qualifying campaigns and friendlies mix the levels, which is what makes the ratings comparable at all.

Neutral venues are dropped

The model carries a single home advantage and has no way to say a match had none. Fitting a tournament final on neutral ground as though the nominal home side had one would bias that parameter for every other match, so those {{ nations.n_neutral_dropped }} matches are excluded instead of mismodelled.

The decay is slower than the club one

The divisions decay a result with a {{ half_life }}-day half-life, tuned for clubs playing 38 matches a season. A national side plays about ten a year, so that setting would leave a rating standing on almost nothing. This model uses {{ nations.half_life }} days, chosen by holdout in scripts/tune_nations.py.

What that number is not. It is not comparable with anything on the scorecard or with the backtest. Those grade club matches against a closing line; this grades international matches against nothing but the coin flip. The only thing the two share is the 1.0986 baseline, and a model can clear that comfortably while still losing to the market by a distance — which is exactly what the club model does. Beating guessing is a low bar, and it is the only bar this page has cleared.

The honest comparison is the closing line, and it is missing here because grading these matches against the closing price is real work that has not been done. Until it is, the right number of claims to make about this model's accuracy against a market is zero.

The decay above was chosen from a flat region, not a sharp optimum: every value between {{ nations_holdout.flat_from }} and {{ nations_holdout.flat_to }} per day scored within 0.0005 nats of the best, well inside the sampling error. What the holdout does separate clearly is the club setting from all of them. Reproduce it with {{ nations_holdout.script }}.
